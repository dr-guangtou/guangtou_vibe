# Skill: Nynke Tynagel–Style Object Tableau Illustration

## Purpose
Use this skill to:
- generate new illustrations that feel plausibly adjacent to Nynke Tynagel’s visual language
- convert a photo or scene into a Tynagel-like graphic treatment
- art-direct human or AI image generation toward the right composition, palette, and object logic
- evaluate whether an image is close enough to the target style

This skill is not for copying a specific artwork. It is for capturing the reusable visual grammar behind the body of work.

---

## Style summary
A **frontal, object-dense, iconographic illustration style** built from **crisp dark outlines, flat high-contrast color fields, shallow space, and tightly organized compositions**. Ordinary objects are elevated into clear visual emblems and arranged with almost architectural order. The work feels **realistic in content**, but **graphic in execution**: part vernacular still life, part cabinet of curiosities, part poster, part vector illustration.

A useful mental model is:

> **graphic vernacular realism + curated clutter + vector precision + ornamental density**

---

## Why this description is accurate
Across the artist’s own site and profile texts, the most stable recurring traits are:
- **bold, expressive, iconographic imagery**
- **visual fields that are tightly conceived, carefully placed, contextualized, intense but harmonious**
- a background shaped by **flatness, colour, and graphic abstraction**
- a practice in which common things become **icons** or actors on **two-dimensional stages**
- long-standing reliance on the **vector line**, with Adobe Illustrator used to precisely rescale and recompose forms
- recurring use of dense, brim-filled scenes and occasionally a deliberate **central blank field** framed by surrounding objects
- narrative construction through imagined characters, environments, and their possessions

These points should be treated as the primary anchor for the style.

---

## Core visual DNA

### 1. Subject matter: the material world as cast of characters
The imagery usually starts from **things** rather than landscapes or atmospheric scenes. Prefer:
- household objects
- kitchen tools
- workshop tools
- vehicle parts
- travel objects
- books, papers, cords, bottles, plants
- art and antique objects
- cleaning supplies
- toys, stationery, containers, hardware

Each item should feel like an **archetypal version** of itself: simplified, clarified, recognizable, and visually satisfying.

### 2. Composition: dense but highly controlled
The composition should feel **full**, often nearly packed to the edges, but never randomly messy. Use:
- frontal or near-orthographic viewpoints
- shallow depth
- shelf logic, wall logic, tabletop logic, cabinet logic, garage logic, camper-van logic
- object clustering with clear local neighborhoods
- a strong center of organization
- optional **blank central rectangle** or empty working area, framed by objects on all sides

Important: the image should feel **collected and curated**, not merely cluttered.

### 3. Object treatment: realistic proportions, graphic simplification
Objects are not loose cartoons. They are:
- proportionally believable
- reduced to clean silhouettes
- partitioned into legible planar zones
- outlined consistently
- detailed enough to be identifiable instantly

The realism lives in **selection, proportion, and specificity**, not in painterly modeling.

### 4. Line language: vector discipline
The line is one of the clearest signatures.
- use crisp, stable contour lines
- prefer near-uniform dark outlines
- keep internal detail lines intentional and economical
- no scratchy sketch marks
- no brushy contour variation unless deliberately adapted

Think **Adobe Illustrator logic**, even when the final image is painted or printed.

### 5. Color: bold, flat, high-contrast, controlled
Use a palette of:
- saturated primaries and secondaries
- strong reds, oranges, yellows, teals, blues, greens
- black, white, cream, pale gray, warm beige as structure
- occasional navy or dark grounds for poster-like contrast

Color should behave as **flat local color**. Avoid photographic gradients. Contrast comes from adjacency and shape separation.

### 6. Space: compressed and stage-like
Depth is limited. Objects may overlap, but the image still reads like a **constructed two-dimensional stage**. This is crucial.
- avoid deep cinematic perspective
- avoid dramatic foreshortening
- avoid atmospheric haze
- compress background and foreground into a unified decorative field

### 7. Mood: playful, observant, slightly obsessive, materially affectionate
The pictures often feel joyful, curious, and visually abundant. Even when the subject is mundane, the treatment is celebratory.

## Composition grammar
Use the following grammar when constructing an image.

### A. Base structure
Choose one compositional scaffold:
1. **Framed void** — blank central area, objects around edges
2. **Shelf tableau** — stacked shelves or ledges loaded with objects
3. **Workbench / kitchen / garage wall** — practical setting filled with tools and containers
4. **Vehicle or room façade** — frontal view of a van, cabinet, kitchen, or wall section
5. **Collector’s cabinet** — symmetrical or near-symmetrical accumulation of mixed treasures

### B. Object density
Aim for **high density with high legibility**.
- objects may touch or nearly touch
- gaps are deliberate, not accidental
- avoid large dead zones unless they are the central blank field
- every edge should contribute to the composition

### C. Hierarchy
Organize the scene in 3 layers:
- **anchor forms**: major structural objects such as vehicle, cabinet, large appliance, shelving, easel, table
- **secondary forms**: tools, books, containers, decor, household items
- **micro-accents**: labels, cords, hooks, fruit, rulers, notes, flowers, switches, sockets, clamps, handles

### D. Rhythms
Build visual rhythm with:
- repeated circular forms
- repeated rectangular modules
- hanging cords and chains
- stripes, checks, labels, grilles, shelving slats
- alternating warm and cool accents

### E. Cropping
Tight cropping is good. Let the composition feel slightly overfull.

---

## Formal rules

### Required
- sharp object boundaries
- dark contour lines
- flat or nearly flat color regions
- frontal or shallow-angle viewpoint
- dense arrangement of recognizable objects
- explicit geometry
- high legibility at both full image and zoomed-in scale

### Encouraged
- a central empty plane surrounded by objects
- ordinary objects treated with unusual dignity
- a subtle narrative implied by what the owner uses or collects
- mixture of utility and beauty
- juxtaposition of the banal and the precious

### Avoid
- painterly brush texture
- watercolor softness
- photographic depth of field
- glossy CGI rendering
- messy expressive distortion
- loose comic exaggeration
- surreal shape melting
- muddy tonal shading
- strong directional cinematic light
- chaotic clutter with weak edge definition

---

## Texture and shading rules
Texture should be sparse and strategic.
- default to **flat fills**
- use minimal tonal shifts only when needed to separate adjacent forms
- use tiny highlights or shadows sparingly
- decorative patterns are welcome if they remain geometric and controlled
- surfaces should feel printed, lacquered, enamelled, cut-paper-like, or vector-constructed

Do **not** rely on realistic material rendering to create interest. Interest should come from shape, adjacency, and object inventory.

---

## Narrative logic
A strong Tynagel-like image often suggests a person without showing the person directly.

Use this question:

> Who owns these objects, and what do the chosen objects reveal about their routines, tastes, obsessions, or profession?

This helps produce images that feel curated rather than generic.

Examples:
- an astronomer’s chaotic-but-beautiful office wall
- a ceramic artist’s van packed for summer travel
- a conservator’s workbench with tools, notes, solvents, brushes, and framed artifacts
- a collector couple’s salon wall of antiques and design pieces

---

## Prompting strategy

### Base generation prompt
Use this as a neutral foundation:

> Create a dense frontal illustration of [SCENE] using crisp dark outlines, flat high-contrast color fields, shallow space, and tightly organized object groupings. Treat everyday objects as iconic emblems: simplified but proportionally believable, highly legible, and arranged with architectural order. Use a bold controlled palette with reds, oranges, yellows, blues, teals, greens, black, white, and warm neutrals. Keep the composition packed yet harmonious, decorative yet realistic, like a curated cabinet of things or a perfectly composed cluttered interior. Minimal shading, no painterly texture, no cinematic lighting, no atmospheric blur.

### Enhanced generation prompt with compositional control
> Frontal, near-orthographic object tableau of [SCENE]. Build the image around [CENTRAL VOID / VEHICLE FRONT / SHELVES / TABLETOP / CABINET]. Fill the surrounding field with carefully arranged everyday objects, each drawn as a crisp icon with uniform dark contour lines and flat saturated color blocks. Keep the scene dense, balanced, and materially affectionate. Shallow depth, vector-like precision, geometric partitions, highly legible silhouettes, curated clutter, decorative realism, poster-like clarity.

### Style-transfer prompt
> Transform this image into a highly organized object-dense graphic illustration. Preserve the recognizable objects and overall scene, but rebuild them with crisp dark outlines, flat bold color fields, shallow frontal space, and explicit geometric boundaries. Remove photographic lighting and painterly texture. Emphasize iconographic clarity, curated arrangement, and vector-like precision. Make the composition dense yet harmonious, with everyday objects treated as visual emblems.

### Negative prompt
> avoid watercolor, oil painting, airbrush gradients, cinematic lighting, photorealism, 3D CGI shine, blurry edges, deep perspective, loose sketch lines, expressive brushwork, anime stylization, manga screentones, surreal distortion, messy clutter, soft focus, muddy palette

## Scene recipes

### Recipe 1: Blank-canvas tableau
Best for posters and “framed void” compositions.
- Place a large blank rectangle in the center
- Surround it with dense, useful, character-specific objects
- Keep edges active on all four sides
- Use the blank field as breathing room and focal anchor

### Recipe 2: Vehicle / machine façade
Best for vans, cars, workshop fronts, appliances.
- Use a frontal view of the main object as the anchor
- Fill margins with tools, cables, fluids, accessories, labels, parts
- Keep everything diagrammatic and packed

### Recipe 3: Curio cabinet
Best for collector scenes.
- Mix art objects with humble domestic items
- Use bilateral balance loosely, not rigidly
- Vary scales deliberately
- Let each object feel collectible

### Recipe 4: Kitchen / domestic workstation
Best for domestic realism.
- Organize around shelves, stove, counters, fridge, table, hooks
- Use bottles, vegetables, towels, cookware, cups, brushes, notes, cleaning products
- Keep the domestic clutter affectionate, not dirty or chaotic

### Recipe 5: Professional habitat portrait
Best when implying a person through their possessions.
- Pick a profession or personality
- Select 20–60 telling objects
- Arrange them so the scene becomes a portrait-by-inventory

## Adaptation rules for other subjects
To apply the style to a new image, preserve these invariants:
1. **object clarity over atmosphere**
2. **flat graphic logic over photoreal tonal logic**
3. **frontal organization over immersive perspective**
4. **compositional density over sparse minimalism**
5. **curation over randomness**

You may change:
- subject matter
- palette bias
- degree of symmetry
- whether the scene is domestic, mechanical, artistic, scientific, or commercial

You should not change:
- crisp contour logic
- graphic flattening
- dense organized object ecology
- strong shape separation

---

## Failure modes
The style has drifted too far if the result becomes:
- too painterly
- too soft or atmospheric
- too comic-book exaggerated
- too 3D or glossy
- too empty or minimalist
- too chaotic without compositional order
- too textural instead of structural
- too dependent on dramatic light instead of color and line

---

## Evaluation checklist
Score each item from 1 to 5.

- Are the objects instantly legible?
- Are the boundaries crisp and controlled?
- Is the palette bold and flat rather than tonal and painterly?
- Does the space feel shallow and stage-like?
- Is the composition dense but deliberate?
- Do the objects feel curated and narratively chosen?
- Does the image read as decorative realism rather than naturalistic depiction?
- Could the image plausibly function as both artwork and poster/design graphic?

A strong match usually scores 4 or 5 on most items.

---

## Short reusable style block
Use this when you need a compact style tag:

> Dense frontal object tableau; crisp dark outlines; flat saturated color; shallow space; vector-like precision; iconographic everyday objects; curated clutter; geometric partitions; decorative realism; poster clarity; high legibility; minimal shading.

---

## Long reusable style block
Use this when model guidance needs to be stronger:

> Illustrate the scene as a tightly organized, object-dense tableau with a frontal or near-orthographic viewpoint and shallow compressed space. Render all objects with crisp dark contour lines, highly legible geometric silhouettes, and flat high-contrast color fields rather than painterly modeling. Treat ordinary objects as iconic emblems and arrange them with architectural care, as if constructing a cabinet of curiosities, workshop wall, kitchen inventory, or collector’s display. The composition should feel packed to the edges but still harmonious, with every object clearly bounded and intentionally placed. Prefer bold reds, oranges, yellows, blues, teals, greens, black, white, and warm neutrals. Minimal shading, no atmospheric effects, no soft focus, no loose sketchiness.

## Source anchors used to build this skill
These source points were especially important:
- The artist’s own “About” text describes the imagery as **bold**, **expressive**, **iconographic**, **heraldic and pop cartoonish**, with **strong narrative quality**, and compositions that are **tightly conceived**, **carefully placed**, and **intense but harmonious**.
- The Maharam profile identifies the artist’s upbringing around **flatness, colour, and graphic abstraction**.
- The Maharam profile says common daily objects are transformed into **icons**, and even small trivial objects become actors on **two-dimensional stages**.
- The same profile notes that **the vector line** has long been her tool of choice, with Adobe Illustrator used to precisely rescale and recompose illustrations.
- The PAN project text explains that a scene may be constructed by imagining a **character** and then portraying that character through their environment and possessions.
- The Maharam profile explicitly frames the PAN image as a **fictitious wunderkammer**, which helps define the cabinet-like, collector-like compositional logic.
- The Maharam profile also explains the **Blank Canvas** works as compositions that create imaginative space through a deliberate empty area embedded inside the material world.
